# Top 15 Super Natural Experiences in Columbus — Under $15 Each

Outstanding outdoor moments in and around Columbus without spending much. Each pick is free or caps at about $15 per person for the core experience (parking meters and snacks extra).

## How to use

1. Pick **one** experience for a free afternoon — or chain two nearby.
2. Check hours/alerts the morning of (flooding, trail closures, heat).
3. Bring water, bug spray in summer, and a layer year-round.

**Price key:** Free · $ · $$ (still ≤ $15 for the listed experience)

---

## The 15

### 1. Highbanks Metro Park — overlook + river trails — Free
Bluff views over the Olentangy, oak forests, and enough loop options for 45–120 minutes.  
**Go for:** golden hour at the overlook.  
**Tip:** Start at the nature center lot; stay on marked trails near edges.

### 2. Battelle Darby Creek Metro Park — prairie + bison overlook — Free
Wide sky, tallgrass, and (when present) bison viewing from designated areas.  
**Go for:** late spring wildflowers or fall color.  
**Tip:** Pair a short prairie walk with the creek corridor if energy remains.

### 3. Scioto Mile / downtown riverfront walk — Free
Wide paths, fountains, bridges, and skyline without a ticket.  
**Go for:** sunset from the river steps.  
**Tip:** Park once south of downtown or rideshare in; walk north along the water.

### 4. Sharon Woods Metro Park — lake loop — Free
Easy multi-use loop around the lake; ducks, shade, and a reset after a long week.  
**Go for:** morning walk before it warms up.  
**Tip:** Bring binoculars for water birds; keep dogs leashed.

### 5. Inniswood Metro Gardens — seasonal gardens — Free / $
Woodland gardens, themed beds, and quiet paths inside Westerville’s metro system.  
**Go for:** spring blooms or fall foliage.  
**Tip:** Confirm any special-event pricing; everyday garden stroll is typically free/low.

### 6. Franklin Park Conservatory exterior gardens + park grounds — $–$$
You can enjoy surrounding park space and often catch exterior beauty without a full indoor ticket — or buy timed entry when you want the glasshouse (watch for deals under $15).  
**Go for:** cherry blossoms / lights seasons when priced in budget.  
**Tip:** Check current gate price the morning of; walk the park loops either way.

### 7. Griggs Reservoir / riverside path — Free
Water, boats, and a simple paved stretch for legs and lungs.  
**Go for:** weekday late afternoon.  
**Tip:** Watch for cyclists; use marked access points.

### 8. Three Creeks Metro Park — confluence trails — Free
Where creeks meet — flat trails, birds, and “you’re still in the city” surprise.  
**Go for:** after rain (careful of mud) when water is lively.  
**Tip:** Waterproof shoes if the towpath is wet.

### 9. Blendon Woods Metro Park — forest + bird blind — Free
Mature woods and a bird-watching blind when you want quiet over spectacle.  
**Go for:** migration seasons (spring/fall mornings).  
**Tip:** Move slowly near the blind; silence pays off.

### 10. Glacier Ridge Metro Park — open fields + wind — Free
Big-sky metro park northwest of town — meadows, paths, and room to breathe.  
**Go for:** kite weather or clear winter days.  
**Tip:** Little shade — sunscreen and water are not optional in summer.

### 11. Antrim Park — lake loop (Worthington edge) — Free
Neighborhood-scale nature: lake circuit, geese, and an easy “I only have 40 minutes” win.  
**Go for:** lunch-break loop.  
**Tip:** Crowded after work — go early if you want calm.

### 12. Blacklick Woods Metro Park — Beech-Maple trails — Free
Classic central Ohio forest floor — roots, canopy, and mushroom season curiosity (look, don’t pick unless you’re trained).  
**Go for:** after a rain when the woods smell alive.  
**Tip:** Trails can be slick; wear grip shoes.

### 13. Hoover Reservoir — shoreline walk / birding — Free
Water + open light on the northeast side; herons and weekend fishermen energy.  
**Go for:** sunrise if you can swing it.  
**Tip:** Multiple access points — pick one and don’t try the whole reservoir in one go.

### 14. Hayden Falls — short gorge payoff — Free
A quick walk to a local waterfall pocket when you want “nature drama” without a long drive.  
**Go for:** after steady rain (stronger flow).  
**Tip:** Stairs/terrain — not a stroller stroll; keep kids close.

### 15. Olentangy Trail segment (north) — Free
Long paved trail for bikes or a purposeful walk under trees along the river corridor.  
**Go for:** a 5-mile out-and-back from a single parking pin.  
**Tip:** Screenshot your start pin; the trail looks similar for miles.

## Weather / season swaps

| Condition | Do this |
| --- | --- |
| Heat advisory | Shade parks early (Blendon, Blacklick); skip open prairie midday |
| Ice / freeze-thaw | Stick to paved Scioto Mile / Antrim; avoid gorge stairs |
| Flooding | Skip creek-bottom trails; choose overlooks (Highbanks) |
| Peak fall weekends | Arrive at open or pick less famous loops (Glacier Ridge, Three Creeks) |
