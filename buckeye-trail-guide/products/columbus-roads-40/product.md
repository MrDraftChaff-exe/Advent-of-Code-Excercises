# Columbus Roads: Top 20 to Avoid + Top 20 to Use

A free field guide for drivers in and around Columbus, Ohio. Twenty corridors that often cost you time or stress — and twenty better options when the goal is a calmer trip.

## How this list was built

This is a **curated practical list**, not an official ranking. Each pick was weighed using five inputs:

1. **Road quality** — pavement condition, lane drops, awkward merges, and “feels worn down” stretches drivers talk about often  
2. **Maintenance / construction** — recurring work zones and multi-year rebuilds (especially Innerbelt / Crossroads phases)  
3. **Accident patterns** — publicly discussed high-crash corridors and intersections (MORPC / ODPS / ODOT reporting over time)  
4. **Travel time** — peak-hour delay, weekend retail crush, and event-day backups  
5. **User input** — common local advice (what friends, coworkers, and long-time drivers actually avoid or recommend)

**Important:** Crash lists and construction change. Check [ODOT OHGO](https://ohgo.com/), city alerts, and your maps app the morning of. Send corrections or “this got better / worse” notes to **businesshouseholder@gmail.com** — future editions will fold that feedback in.

## Quick rules before you drive

- [ ] Peek at OHGO / maps for crashes and lane closures  
- [ ] Add 15–25 minutes for Innerbelt, Morse, Polaris, and campus-event windows  
- [ ] Prefer one calm arterial over three “shortcut” cuts through neighborhoods  
- [ ] If you’re unfamiliar with downtown one-ways, stay on the freeway outer belt until you’re closer

## Top 20 roads to avoid (when you can)

### 1. I-70 / I-71 downtown overlap (Innerbelt / Crossroads)
**Why:** Chronic congestion + high crash share for its length; multi-year rebuild phases.  
**Worst times:** Weekday peaks, Friday afternoons, bad weather, any crash.  
**Try instead:** I-270 around for through trips; I-670 when it still moves.

### 2. I-71 East Trench / downtown east rebuild zones
**Why:** Active construction, lane shifts, and surprise merges.  
**Worst times:** Weekday mornings into downtown; overnight work bursts.  
**Try instead:** Surface one-ways (Long / Spring / Broad) for short hops once you’re already downtown.

### 3. SR-315 through the downtown / north crush
**Why:** Volume + limited escape lanes; backups stack fast after one incident.  
**Worst times:** Weekday peaks; event nights.  
**Try instead:** Riverside Drive (slower, calmer) or Kenny / Olentangy River Road segments when they fit your start/end.

### 4. Morse Road (retail spine, especially Karl → Cleveland)
**Why:** Dense driveways, turning conflicts, and historically busy crash intersections.  
**Worst times:** Lunch, after-work, Saturday midday.  
**Try instead:** Parallel east-west options north/south of Morse for through trips (Agler / Innis / Schrock depending on end point).

### 5. Cleveland Avenue (key crash intersections)
**Why:** Long arterial with repeated high-crash junctions in regional lists over the years.  
**Worst times:** Peaks and dark/wet evenings.  
**Try instead:** I-71 for longer north-south moves; local collectors for short neighborhood hops.

### 6. East Broad Street (high-injury network stretches)
**Why:** On the city’s Vision Zero high-injury focus; busy, wide, and historically risky.  
**Worst times:** Peaks; late-night speeding windows.  
**Note:** Safety demo projects have been changing the street — still drive extra careful.  
**Try instead:** Main / Town / Livingston for some east-side trips; I-70 for longer east-west.

### 7. East Livingston Avenue (esp. near Brice / Hamilton)
**Why:** High-volume intersections that repeatedly show up in regional crash discussions.  
**Worst times:** After-work eastbound, weekends.  
**Try instead:** Main Street / Refugee connectors or I-70 when heading farther out.

### 8. SR-161 / Dublin-Granville Road (retail & ramp chaos)
**Why:** Shopping access + freeway ramps = stop-and-go and side-swipe risk.  
**Worst times:** Evenings and weekends near big retail.  
**Try instead:** I-270 for distance; Wilson Bridge / Worthington locals for short trips.

### 9. Hilliard Rome Road (Renner / retail cluster)
**Why:** Turn conflicts and peak backups at major junctions.  
**Worst times:** Weekday evenings, Saturday midday.  
**Try instead:** I-70 for longer moves; Trabue / Fishinger for west-side crosstown.

### 10. Frank Road / Refugee / Winchester Pike area
**Why:** Complex junctions and freight + local mix.  
**Worst times:** Peaks and truck-heavy midday.  
**Try instead:** Alum Creek Drive / Gender Road for many SE patterns.

### 11. Sawmill Road (Dublin–Worthington retail corridor)
**Why:** Constant driveway friction and light-to-light delay.  
**Worst times:** Lunch and after-work.  
**Try instead:** Hard Road / Emerald Parkway / Avery–Cosgray for many NW trips.

### 12. Polaris Parkway (weekend retail crush)
**Why:** Destination traffic overwhelms the corridor.  
**Worst times:** Saturday midday–afternoon; holiday weeks.  
**Try instead:** I-71 to outer Polaris access points; arrive earlier or park once.

### 13. High Street (campus / Short North event windows)
**Why:** Pedestrian density, event surges, and stop-start blocks.  
**Worst times:** Game days, festival nights, weekend evenings.  
**Try instead:** Parallel north-south streets a few blocks over; rideshare for the last mile.

### 14. I-270 NW arc (Dublin–Worthington) at peaks
**Why:** Through traffic + local on/off ramps; one crash freezes the belt.  
**Worst times:** Weekday AM/PM.  
**Try instead:** Hard / Emerald / Sawmill only if your trip is short and local.

### 15. I-670 downtown merges
**Why:** Short weaves and sudden slowdowns.  
**Worst times:** Peaks and airport rushes.  
**Try instead:** Stay on I-270 longer for airport trips when maps show 670 jammed.

### 16. Bethel Road (retail stretch)
**Why:** Driveways + peaks = slow and fender-bender friendly.  
**Worst times:** Lunch and after-work.  
**Try instead:** Henderson / Godown / local collectors for neighborhood trips.

### 17. Olentangy River Road (north / campus-adjacent peaks)
**Why:** Commuter volume and limited passing room in places.  
**Worst times:** Weekday peaks.  
**Try instead:** Kenny Road or SR-315 only when it’s actually moving; bike/transit for short campus hops.

### 18. Hamilton Road (south of Easton, peak windows)
**Why:** Retail + apartment density; left turns stack up.  
**Worst times:** Evenings and Saturdays.  
**Try instead:** I-270 for longer runs; parallel collectors for local errands.

### 19. Tuttle Crossing / mall access roads
**Why:** Destination spikes and short merges.  
**Worst times:** Weekend afternoons and holidays.  
**Try instead:** Enter from the less obvious side roads; shop off-peak.

### 20. Lane Avenue / campus-edge connectors (event days)
**Why:** Event surge + limited outlets.  
**Worst times:** Stadium / arena arrival and exit waves.  
**Try instead:** Park farther out and walk/rideshare; leave 30+ minutes early.

## Top 20 roads to use (smarter defaults)

### 1. I-270 (outer belt) for through trips
**Why:** Usually beats fighting downtown when you’re not stopping downtown.  
**Best for:** Airport ↔ suburbs, north ↔ south without Innerbelt pain.

### 2. I-670 (when it’s clear)
**Why:** Fast downtown–airport and east-side connector — until it isn’t.  
**Best for:** Airport runs outside peak crash chaos.

### 3. Riverside Drive
**Why:** Calmer parallel to SR-315 for many north–south local trips.  
**Best for:** Scenic, lower-stress driving when time isn’t critical.

### 4. Kenny Road
**Why:** Useful north-side parallel when Olentangy / 315 are stacked.  
**Best for:** Worthington / north Columbus local travel.

### 5. Hard Road + Emerald Parkway
**Why:** Planned suburban arterials that often beat Sawmill stop-and-go.  
**Best for:** Dublin / NW errands and office parks.

### 6. Avery Road / Cosgray Road
**Why:** Western Dublin connectors with fewer driveway fights than Sawmill.  
**Best for:** NW ↔ west local trips.

### 7. Trabue Road / Fishinger Road
**Why:** Solid west-side east–west movement without Hilliard Rome retail crush.  
**Best for:** Hilliard / UA / west Columbus.

### 8. Alum Creek Drive
**Why:** Predictable SE spine for freight and locals.  
**Best for:** Rickenbacker / SE industrial and south-east suburbs.

### 9. Gender Road
**Why:** Canal Winchester / SE connector that often beats Livingston chaos.  
**Best for:** SE suburbs ↔ I-70 area.

### 10. McNaughten Road / Noe-Bixby Road
**Why:** East-side alternatives when Livingston is ugly.  
**Best for:** Reynoldsburg / east Columbus local runs.

### 11. Schrock Road
**Why:** Westerville / NE belt alternative to Morse for many trips.  
**Best for:** NE crosstown without the Morse retail gauntlet.

### 12. Agler Road / Innis Road (as Morse bypass pieces)
**Why:** Not glamorous — but often move when Morse doesn’t.  
**Best for:** East–west hops north/south of Morse.

### 13. Wilson Bridge Road
**Why:** Worthington connector that avoids some SR-161 retail mess.  
**Best for:** Worthington locals and I-270 access.

### 14. Spring Street / Long Street (downtown one-ways)
**Why:** Designed for downtown flow once you know the grid.  
**Best for:** Short downtown hops — learn them before peak hour.

### 15. Goodale Street / Nationwide Blvd area
**Why:** Useful Arena District / downtown north edge movement.  
**Best for:** Short downtown / near-north trips.

### 16. Third Street / Front Street (downtown N–S when clear)
**Why:** Often cleaner than High Street during events.  
**Best for:** Downtown north–south without the High Street crowd.

### 17. Broad Street west of downtown (for short local trips)
**Why:** Can beat a freeway hop for short distances — watch Vision Zero zones east.  
**Best for:** Near-west / Hilltop local.

### 18. Main Street (select east-side segments)
**Why:** Parallel option when Broad / Livingston are jammed.  
**Best for:** Near East / Bexley-adjacent patterns (watch local speed limits).

### 19. Easton outer loop roads (not the core crush)
**Why:** Edge access beats fighting the center lots and Morse feeders.  
**Best for:** Easton trips — park once, walk the rest.

### 20. Neighborhood collectors + timed lights (your “local knowledge” roads)
**Why:** The best road is often the one two blocks off the arterial.  
**Best for:** Repeat trips you’ve tested at your real departure time.
