# Top 30 Fishing Spots Around Columbus, Ohio

Thirty places to wet a line within an easy drive of Columbus. Bring a valid **Ohio fishing license** (and any required stamps), then use the local tip at each spot for parking, nearby food, and what to do if the fish aren’t cooperating.

## Before you go

- [ ] Current Ohio fishing license (ODNR) — phone download counts if accepted; bring backup ID
- [ ] Know statewide size/bag limits for what you’re targeting (they change — check ODNR before you keep fish)
- [ ] Rod + reel you trust, spare hooks/soft plastics, needle-nose pliers
- [ ] Landing net for bigger water
- [ ] Sunscreen, water, bug spray (summer), warm layers (shoulder seasons)
- [ ] Trash bag — pack out what you pack in
- [ ] Optional: small cooler, folding chair, polarized sunglasses

**License note:** This guide assumes you already have (or will get) a legal Ohio license. It is not a substitute for ODNR regulations.

**How to use:** Circle 2–3 spots near each other, pick a half-day window, and leave if pressure or weather turns.

## Reservoirs & big water

### 1. Hoover Reservoir — Westerville / NE Columbus
**Go for:** Crappie, bass, catfish, and shoreline casting with room to roam.  
**Local tip:** Multiple access points — pick one arm and fish it well instead of driving the whole lake. Westerville has coffee and quick food 10 minutes from most ramps.  
**Nearby:** Blendon Woods / Inniswood if the bite dies and you want a short walk.

### 2. Alum Creek Lake — Lewis Center / Delaware County
**Go for:** Bass, saugeye, and boat or bank fishing on a big Corps reservoir.  
**Local tip:** Weekday mornings beat weekend jet-ski traffic. Confirm which lots and ramps are open seasonally.  
**Nearby:** Lewis Center / Polaris corridor for food after you load the truck.

### 3. Griggs Reservoir — NW Columbus (Upper Arlington / Hilliard edge)
**Go for:** Urban reservoir fishing without leaving the city.  
**Local tip:** Watch for kayakers and cyclists on shared paths; keep gear tidy near the water.  
**Nearby:** Riverside parks and UA/Hilliard lunch spots along Fishinger / Trabue corridors.

### 4. O’Shaughnessy Reservoir — Dublin / NW
**Go for:** Bass and panfish with scenic Scioto corridor views.  
**Local tip:** Wind can push hard down the lake — fish the protected pockets when it’s gusty.  
**Nearby:** Dublin Bridge Park / Historic Dublin for a post-fish walk and coffee.

### 5. Buckeye Lake — SE of Columbus (fair day-trip)
**Go for:** Classic central Ohio lake fishing with lots of shoreline variety.  
**Local tip:** Summer weekends get crowded — dawn or weekday if you can. Confirm public access vs private docks.  
**Nearby:** Small-town food around the lake communities; don’t count on every dock being public.

### 6. Delaware Lake — Delaware State Park (north)
**Go for:** A quieter reservoir day than Alum Creek on some weekdays.  
**Local tip:** Pair fishing with a park picnic; check state park launch fees/hours.  
**Nearby:** Delaware downtown for a sit-down meal on the way home.

### 7. Deer Creek Lake — Deer Creek State Park (SW day-trip)
**Go for:** Bigger-water feel without driving to Lake Erie.  
**Local tip:** Plan it as a half-day or full day — it’s farther than metro ponds. Gas up before you leave Columbus.  
**Nearby:** Park camp/picnic areas; limited “city” food nearby so pack snacks.

### 8. Madison Lake — Madison Lake State Park (SW)
**Go for:** Smaller state-park lake that’s easier to read than huge reservoirs.  
**Local tip:** Great “teach a friend to cast” water — less overwhelming than Hoover.  
**Nearby:** Pack a cooler; amenities are park-basic.

## Metro Parks lakes & ponds

### 9. Sharon Woods Metro Park — lake loop
**Go for:** Convenient NE Columbus fishing with easy parking.  
**Local tip:** Share the shore with walkers — keep casts away from the path. Early morning is calmer.  
**Nearby:** Park restrooms + Westerville food after.

### 10. Antrim Park — Worthington
**Go for:** Quick after-work casts on a neighborhood lake.  
**Local tip:** Geese and foot traffic are part of the deal; travel light.  
**Nearby:** Worthington / High Street bites within a few minutes.

### 11. Rocky Fork Metro Park — lake / shoreline
**Go for:** Metro Park water with room to try a few banks.  
**Local tip:** Check park rules for fishing zones and any seasonal closures.  
**Nearby:** Gahanna / New Albany corridors for food.

### 12. Pickerington Ponds Metro Park
**Go for:** Wetland ponds and bird-heavy quiet — fishing plus binoculars.  
**Local tip:** Soft banks after rain; wear shoes you don’t mind muddy.  
**Nearby:** Pickerington for groceries/snacks on the way out.

### 13. Glacier Ridge Metro Park — ponds / open water edges
**Go for:** Northwest metro fishing when you want sky and fewer downtown vibes.  
**Local tip:** Little shade — summer heat is real; bring water.  
**Nearby:** Dublin / Plain City direction for food depending which entrance you use.

### 14. Prairie Oaks Metro Park — Big Darby corridor
**Go for:** Creek/river energy inside the metro system (check posted fishing rules).  
**Local tip:** Current and clarity change after storms — wait a day if it’s chocolate milk.  
**Nearby:** West-side / Hilliard food; enjoy the prairie walk if fishing slows.

### 15. Battelle Darby Creek Metro Park
**Go for:** Creek fishing and prairie backdrop — different pace than reservoirs.  
**Local tip:** Stay out of restricted/sensitive zones; fish only where posted as allowed.  
**Nearby:** Nature center area for orientation; pack lunch.

### 16. Three Creeks Metro Park — confluence water
**Go for:** Urban creek junctions with surprising wildlife.  
**Local tip:** After heavy rain, currents spike — safety first, ego second.  
**Nearby:** South/east Columbus food corridors; easy “I’ll just fish 90 minutes” stop.

### 17. Blacklick Woods Metro Park — ponds/creek edges
**Go for:** Forest-feel fishing close to Reynoldsburg / east side.  
**Local tip:** Roots and mud — grip shoes beat flip-flops.  
**Nearby:** East-side fast casual on Brice / Main corridors.

### 18. Scioto Audubon Metro Park — downtown-adjacent water
**Go for:** Skyline fishing energy (follow all posted rules).  
**Local tip:** Great for “I only have an hour after work” — park once, travel light.  
**Nearby:** Brewery District / German Village bites after you rinse your hands.

### 19. Homestead Park — Hilliard (pond)
**Go for:** Family-friendly local pond fishing.  
**Local tip:** Ideal for kids’ first bluegill day; keep expectations small and fun.  
**Nearby:** Hilliard Rome / Old Hilliard food.

### 20. Darby Creek / park access points (metro west)
**Go for:** Moving water when lakes feel stagnant.  
**Local tip:** Respect private property — use official park access only.  
**Nearby:** Combine with a short prairie trail if the bite is slow.

## Rivers & creeks (public access only)

### 21. Scioto River — Griggs to downtown corridor
**Go for:** Urban river fishing with multiple public parks as base camps.  
**Local tip:** Pick one park access (don’t hop fences). Summer evenings can be excellent.  
**Nearby:** Grandview / Franklinton / downtown food depending where you exit.

### 22. Scioto River — south of downtown (public parks)
**Go for:** Slightly less polished banks for anglers who want space.  
**Local tip:** Watch footing on riprap; tell someone your plan.  
**Nearby:** South Side / German Village for a victory meal.

### 23. Olentangy River — Worthington / Clintonville access
**Go for:** North-side river fishing near neighborhoods you already know.  
**Local tip:** Bike path traffic is high — cast with awareness.  
**Nearby:** Clintonville coffee and casual food on High Street.

### 24. Olentangy River — north toward Delaware County public spots
**Go for:** Stretching upstream when Columbus banks feel pressured.  
**Local tip:** Confirm each lot is public; river corridors mix park and private land.  
**Nearby:** Powell / Delaware food options on the drive.

### 25. Big Walnut Creek — east/southeast public parks
**Go for:** Creek fishing with less “reservoir crowd” energy.  
**Local tip:** After storms, wait for safer levels and clearer water.  
**Nearby:** Gahanna / Reynoldsburg / Canal Winchester depending on which stretch.

### 26. Alum Creek — below-dam / creek sections (public only)
**Go for:** Moving water variety when the lake is blown out by wind.  
**Local tip:** Dam areas can have special rules and dangerous current — read signs.  
**Nearby:** Westerville / Lewis Center for supplies.

### 27. Blacklick Creek — east-side park access
**Go for:** Local creek casting without a long drive.  
**Local tip:** Small water = light tackle and patience.  
**Nearby:** East Columbus shopping corridors for last-minute bait/snacks.

## Easy day-trip & “worth the drive” options

### 28. A.W. Marion State Park — Circleville area
**Go for:** Compact state-park lake south of town.  
**Local tip:** Treat it as a half-day outing; check park hours before you leave.  
**Nearby:** Circleville for a simple meal on the way back.

### 29. Slate Run Metro Park — ponds & quiet park time
**Go for:** Southeast metro outing that feels farther than it is.  
**Local tip:** Confirm fishing-allowed water vs wildlife-only ponds before you cast.  
**Nearby:** Park trails and farm-area scenery if you switch to walking mode.

### 30. Chestnut Ridge Metro Park — perched water / park edges
**Go for:** A change-of-scenery metro stop with fishing potential where posted.  
**Local tip:** Combine with a short ridge walk so the day isn’t only “wait for a bite.”  
**Nearby:** Southeast suburbs for gas and food before you return north.

## Half-day sampler menus

**NE Columbus loop:** Hoover → Sharon Woods → Westerville food  
**NW metro loop:** Griggs or O’Shaughnessy → Dublin coffee → short Bridge Park walk  
**East side loop:** Rocky Fork or Pickerington Ponds → Gahanna/Pickerington bite  
**Teach-a-kid day:** Homestead Park or Madison Lake → pack snacks → quit while it’s still fun  
**Day-trip:** Deer Creek or Delaware Lake — leave early, home by dinner
